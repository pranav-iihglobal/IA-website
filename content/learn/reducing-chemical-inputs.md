Nobody should ask a farmer to gamble a season's income. Switching away from chemicals overnight is a gamble — the soil's biology is not ready to take over. The right way is stepwise: rebuild the biology first, then reduce the chemicals it replaces.

## Season 1: Add, don't subtract

In the first season, change nothing about your chemical program. Just add biology:

- Apply a **bacterial consortium** (nitrogen-fixing, phosphate-solubilizing, potash-mobilizing) with compost or through drip.
- Use a **mycorrhizal inoculant** at sowing or transplanting.
- Return organic matter — farmyard manure, compost, crop residue.

You are hiring the workers before reducing the supplies. Many farmers already see better plant vigour and root growth this season.

## Season 2: Reduce by a quarter

With biology established, cut chemical fertilizer by about **25%** on the treated field:

- If you normally use four bags of urea, use three.
- Keep the biofertilizer applications going — they are now doing that missing bag's work.
- Watch the crop. Leaf colour and growth will tell you if the balance is right.

Keep one strip at your old full dose as a comparison. Numbers convince better than promises.

## Season 3 and beyond: Find your level

Most farmers settle at **40–60% less** chemical fertilizer than they started with, at equal or better yield. Some go further; few need to return to full doses. What changes along the way:

- **Costs fall.** Biofertilizers cost far less than the bags they replace.
- **Quality rises.** Produce grown on biology often stores and tastes better — buyers notice.
- **The soil softens.** Easier ploughing, better water holding, more earthworms.

## Three rules for the journey

1. **Never cut everything at once.** Step by step is safe; all-at-once is a gamble.
2. **Feed the soil every season.** Organic matter is the fuel for the microbes doing your work.
3. **Compare on your own land.** One treated field beside one untreated field teaches more than any advertisement — ours included.
